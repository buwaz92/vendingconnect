Starting a vending business sounds simple: buy a machine, put it somewhere busy, collect the money. In real life, the people who do well are the ones who get a few key things right from the start. This guide walks through them in order.

## 1. Decide what kind of vending you want to run

Before you spend anything, pick your lane:

- **Drinks only.** Easy to stock and simple to manage. Cold drinks sell well in warm weather and at workplaces with physical jobs.
- **Snacks only.** Lower stock cost per item, but you need to watch use-by dates.
- **Combo machines.** Drinks and snacks in one machine. Great for smaller sites where one machine has to do everything.
- **Specialty.** Coffee, fresh food, healthy options, PPE or other niche products. These can pay well, but they're harder to run.

Most new operators start with drinks or a combo machine, because they're the easiest to keep running.

## 2. Find your first location before you buy

This is the step people skip, and it's the most important one. A great machine in a quiet spot earns almost nothing.

Good first locations are usually:

- Warehouses and factories with shift workers
- Offices with 30+ staff and no café nearby
- Gyms, car washes and trade counters
- Apartment buildings with a common area

Ask the site manager how many people are there each day, the hours, and whether there's already a machine or a café. Read our guide on [how to find good vending machine locations](/guides/how-to-find-good-vending-machine-locations/) for a full checklist.

## 3. Choose the right machine

Match the machine to the site, not the other way round. Things to think about:

- **Size and access.** Measure the doorway, the lift and the space. It's a common mistake to buy a machine that won't fit through the door.
- **Card payments.** Most people don't carry cash anymore. A cashless card reader is a must.
- **Fridge type.** For drinks, check the machine keeps a steady cold temperature, especially in summer.
- **Parts and service.** Pick a brand that's easy to get parts for in Australia.

Our guide on [how to choose the right vending machine](/guides/how-to-choose-the-right-vending-machine/) goes into more detail.

## 4. Sort out the business basics

- Get an **ABN** and set up a separate bank account.
- Talk to an accountant about **GST** and what you can claim.
- Get **public liability insurance**. Many sites will ask for it.
- Have the machine's electrics **tested and tagged** where your site requires it.
- Put a simple **agreement** in place with the site owner covering power, access, commission (if any) and how either side can end it.

## 5. Stock smart, not big

Start with proven sellers. Water, popular soft drinks, energy drinks, chips and chocolate bars are a safe base. Then watch your sales for a few weeks and:

- Drop products that don't move
- Add more of what sells out
- Try one or two new items at a time

Buying from a wholesaler or cash-and-carry keeps your costs down. Track your cost per item so you know your real margin.

## 6. Set your prices

Look at what the nearest shop or servo charges, and price close to it. People pay a little more for convenience, but not a lot more. Round numbers like $3.00 or $4.50 make card and coin payments easy.

## 7. Keep it running

A machine that's empty or broken loses money and trust fast. Set up a regular restock run, and learn the basic fixes yourself, like coin jams, stuck products and card reader resets. Our guide to [common vending machine problems and how to fix them](/guides/common-vending-machine-problems-and-fixes/) is a good place to start.

## The short version

Find the location first, buy a machine that suits it, keep it full and keep it working. Grow one site at a time.
