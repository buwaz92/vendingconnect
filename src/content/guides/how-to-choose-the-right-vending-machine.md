The best vending machine isn't the most expensive one. It's the one that suits your site, keeps working, and is easy to get parts for. Here's how to choose.

## Start with the site, not the machine

Before you look at any models, answer these questions about the location:

- How many people are there each day, and when?
- Do they want cold drinks, snacks, or both?
- Is the spot indoors, out of direct sun and rain?
- Is there a standard power point close by?
- How wide are the doors, halls and lifts on the way in?

These answers tell you what size and type of machine you need.

## Drinks, snacks or combo?

| Machine type | Best for | Things to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drinks (glass front or can/bottle) | Warehouses, gyms, hot sites | Fridge has to keep up in summer |
| Snacks (spiral) | Offices, schools, waiting areas | Watch use-by dates |
| Combo | Smaller sites needing one machine | Less room for each product |

If a site has fewer people, a combo machine is usually the smart choice. Big sites often do best with a separate drinks machine and snack machine side by side.

## New vs second-hand

**New machines** cost more but come with a warranty, newer fridges that use less power, and modern payment systems.

**Second-hand or refurbished machines** are cheaper and can be great value, but check:

- That the fridge gets cold and stays cold
- The coin mech, note reader and card reader all work
- Every spiral or tray turns properly
- Parts for that model are still easy to get

A machine that's cheap to buy but hard to repair can cost more in the end.

## Payment options

Most customers now pay by card or phone. At a minimum, your machine should take:

- **Card and phone tap payments** through a cashless reader
- **Coins**, as a backup for people who still use cash

Some readers also send you sales data, so you can see what's selling without visiting the site.

## Power use and running costs

Older fridges can use a lot of power. If the site owner pays the power bill, a newer energy-efficient machine makes your offer easier to accept. Look for LED lighting and good insulation.

## Parts and service in Australia

Before you buy, find out:

- Who sells parts for that brand in Australia
- How long parts take to arrive
- Whether a local technician knows the model

Downtime is lost sales. A machine you can fix fast is worth a lot.

## Quick checklist

- Measured the space and the path in
- Right machine type for the site
- Card reader included or easy to add
- Fridge tested (for drinks)
- Parts available locally
- Fits your budget, including stock
