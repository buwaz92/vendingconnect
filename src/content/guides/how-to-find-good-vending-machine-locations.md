You can have the best machine in the country, but if it's in the wrong spot, it won't sell. Here's how to find locations that actually make money.

## What makes a great location

Look for these four things:

1. **Lots of people.** The more people walking past every day, the better. Staff numbers matter more than building size.
2. **People who stay a while.** Workers on shifts, gym members and people waiting around buy more than people just passing through.
3. **Few other options.** If there's a café or shop next door, your machine will sell less.
4. **Long hours.** Sites that run early mornings, nights or weekends are great, because the shops are often closed.

## Types of sites that usually work well

- Warehouses and distribution centres
- Factories and manufacturing sites
- Offices with 30 or more staff
- Gyms and fitness centres
- Apartment complexes with common areas
- Car yards, workshops and trade counters
- Medical centres and waiting rooms

## Questions to ask the site owner

- How many staff or visitors are here each day?
- What are the opening hours?
- Is there a safe indoor spot with a power point?
- Has there been a machine here before? What happened?
- Who do I contact for access when restocking?
- Is the site expecting a commission?

## Red flags

Walk away, or be careful, if:

- The site has very few people, even if the owner is keen
- There's a café or vending machine already doing well there
- Access is hard, like only on weekdays from 10 to 2
- The spot is outside or in direct sun
- The owner wants a high commission on a quiet site

## How to approach businesses

Keep it simple and focus on what's in it for them:

- Free machine for their staff or visitors
- Restocking and repairs looked after
- No work for them
- Possible commission if the site is busy

A short, friendly visit or call usually works better than a long email.
